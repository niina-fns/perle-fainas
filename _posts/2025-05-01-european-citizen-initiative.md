This paper argues that the lack of efficiency of the ECI does not solely come from the rigid rules and infrastructure of the EU institutions but is also embedded in the socio-technical architecture of the initiative itself. The ECI platform produces a specific mode of governance that constrains rather than enables political agency. Far from being a neutral technology, the website embodies a precise vision of citizenship: one that is individualised, datafied, and limited by strict procedures. The technocratic nature of the platform, added with the rigid legal constraints of the procedure, ultimately constraints the scope of participation. What appears as an opportunity for direct democracy initiatives actually works as an attribute of legitimacy for the EU, to face the criticism of its top-down management. To support this argument, the paper proceeds in three parts. First, I will conduct a technical walkthrough of the ECI platform following the walkthrough method (Light, Burgess, & Duguay 2018), to trace the user experience when using the website. Then, I will analyse the underlying socio-technical governance model of the ECI, focusing on how its design and legal framework together shape a specific model of participation. Finally, I will offer policy recommendations that aims to reimagine the ECI as a more inclusive and dynamic tool to increase participation.

*Paper written as part of the class Digital Governance taught by Dr. Matthews & Dr. Oreglia at KCL*
